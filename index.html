1. Background check first

What We Can Say Factually

This website separates verified facts from student proposal estimates. The original Montefiore Square capital project was officially reported by NYC agencies as an $18.4 million project that expanded and revamped the Harlem public space. Independent project coverage also reports that the redesigned plaza became 44,650 square feet, included a 13,140 square-foot central terraced lawn, 19 benches, and 36 new trees.

Honesty note: Budget numbers for our proposed improvements are student planning estimates, not official city cost estimates. Revenue and 10-year benefit models are projections, not guaranteed profits.

3. The pitch

Montefiore Plaza as a Community Transformation Plan

Our proposal is not only about making the plaza look nicer. It is about turning a public space into a stronger community asset that supports Harlem residents, local businesses, visitors, families, seniors, students, and people with disabilities.

Safer public space

More lighting, active programming, clear sightlines, and steady foot traffic can improve public comfort.

Greener public space

More trees, rain gardens, and permeable surfaces can support heat reduction and stormwater management.

More economic activity

Food kiosks, weekend markets, sponsorships, and cultural events can support local business visibility.

Stronger Harlem identity

Murals, music, spoken word, and public art can make the plaza feel culturally specific rather than generic.

14. FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions for Government, Businesses, and Investors

Is this an official NYC proposal?

No. This is a student redevelopment pitch created for educational purposes. It uses verified public data where available and labels projections clearly.

Why should the city care?

The proposal supports accessibility, public safety, sustainability, local economic activity, and community-centered design.

Why should business owners care?

A more active plaza can increase foot traffic, support food vendors, create event opportunities, and bring more people near local businesses.

Why should investors or sponsors care?

The project creates opportunities for visible sponsorship, public goodwill, vendor partnerships, event branding, and community investment.

How can it cost less than $18.4 million?

The lower number is a student estimate based on phased construction, modular features, partnerships, and targeted upgrades. It is not an official city bid.

How does the project make money back?

Possible sources include vendor permits, sponsored events, markets, nonprofit grants, and business partnerships. Exact revenue would depend on actual management agreements.

How will the plaza stay clean and maintained?

The plan includes maintenance funding, sanitation coordination, vendor rules, landscaping upkeep, and partnerships with schools, nonprofits, and local organizations.

How will the plaza support blind or low-vision users?

The proposal includes tactile paving, high-contrast signs, audio-friendly digital information, screen-reader support, and clear navigation paths.

How will the plaza serve all ages?

It includes family zones, youth Wi-Fi/study areas, adult lunch spaces, senior seating, and accessible routes for people with disabilities.

What is the biggest limitation of this proposal?

Professional implementation would require official cost estimates, licensed engineering review, community board input, city agency approval, and funding commitments.
